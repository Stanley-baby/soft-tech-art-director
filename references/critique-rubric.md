# Critique Rubric

Score each dimension from 1 to 5. Support every score with observable evidence.

## Concept Alignment

- 1: Style is unrelated to the product or AI behavior.
- 3: Some choices support the concept, but most are generic.
- 5: Visual metaphor and interface behavior clearly express what the AI does.

## Distinctiveness

- 1: Indistinguishable from a generic SaaS or AI template.
- 3: One memorable element, but the overall system is familiar.
- 5: A recognizable combination of composition, typography, material, and motion that can become a brand asset.

## Information Hierarchy

- 1: Effects compete with content; reading order is unclear.
- 3: Main hierarchy works but secondary information is inconsistent.
- 5: Attention, grouping, states, and actions are immediately clear.

## Restraint

- 1: Too many effects, colors, containers, or animations.
- 3: Mostly controlled with one or two excessive areas.
- 5: Every visual treatment has a role; neutral space protects comprehension.

## Production Readiness

- 1: Keyframe only; rules cannot extend to other pages.
- 3: Some reusable tokens and components exist, with gaps in states or responsiveness.
- 5: Tokens, components, responsive behavior, states, and accessibility are defined well enough to implement consistently.

## Common failure modes

- Default blue-purple gradient without semantic purpose
- Glass applied to every card
- Glow used as a substitute for hierarchy
- Decorative grids unrelated to layout
- Abstract blobs that overpower product content
- Too many large rounded cards
- Low-contrast text over gradients
- “Premium” styling that removes useful density
- Marketing visual language copied into operational product screens
- Prompt output that names effects but not placement or intensity
